chapter zero

Before all of this…

You were just someone I had seen around.

Pretty, obviously. And in my head, pretty always seemed to come with a certain amount of attitude included — no discount, no exceptions. I'd been wrong-footed enough times before to know better than to test that theory. So I quietly decided you were probably out of bothering-range, said nothing, and went about my day.

And then came the workshop.

the workshop

A room full of tasks, and somewhere in it, you.

It was just logistics that day — who would take which task, who'd end up working with who. Nothing that should have mattered afterward. I ended up picking you for a task, and my first read was simple: she seems nice.

Then came our first real conversation. Still just work — sorting out assignments, nothing more. But somewhere in that ordinary exchange, I noticed something I hadn't expected: talking to you was easy. No performance, no guessing games, none of the guardedness I'd gotten used to with people I'd just met. Just easy. Genuinely sweet, genuinely respectful — and somehow, that stood out more than I thought it would.

I didn't have a crush. Not yet.

I just had a thought that wouldn't leave: I actually like talking to this person.

nothing special at the time

The first little moments.

Her first day. She showed up late, half-expecting trouble for it, and wasn't even sure what her position was supposed to be. I told her to just mention my name to Vivek sir — it'd smooth things over. It did.

A little confusion, a short conversation, one small favor. That was it. Nothing that felt like the beginning of anything. Looking back, though — it was the first time we actually showed up for each other, even in the smallest way.

12–13 august

The space where you usually were.

One day, you just weren't there. And for reasons I couldn't immediately explain, I kept looking around — checking the usual places, half-expecting you to appear. I didn't understand why I was doing it. It wasn't a plan, it was just a reflex.

Eventually I asked Lisinsa, as casually as I could manage, why the work hadn't been given out to her class that day. She said Aradhya was absent.

Wait — why was I actually looking for her?

I didn't call it anything back then. But maybe that was the first quiet sign that your presence had already started to matter more than I'd noticed.

14 august

Then it rained.

You asked, more or less, if I wanted an umbrella. I hadn't planned on opening mine at all — but somehow I had it out and was asking you to come under it before I'd even thought about it.

You didn't come under it. Not even a little.

I also informed you, very seriously, that attendance was compulsory and you simply had to come. A tiny, forgettable exchange in the rain — except it wasn't forgettable at all.

15 AUGUST

The day everything started feeling a little different.

We kept ending up in the same places, over and over, without ever planning it. It got to the point where it stopped feeling random — the kind of day where the universe seems to be nudging two people toward each other, just to see what happens.

Coincidence?

Maybe.

But there were a lot of them that day.

Then came the legendary misunderstanding.

Someone said a quick goodbye to me in passing — nothing to do with you at all. Somehow, it got filed away as a goodbye meant for her. A whole little story, built on absolutely nothing, and both of us still laugh about it.

And then… the bus.

A moment I almost didn't record.

Truthfully, I hesitated — some part of me was convinced this would end up on Instagram and I'd somehow get called into a school office over it. Very serious risk management, at the time.

But honestly… I'm really glad it happened.

15 august, on the bus

15 August became one of those days I knew I would remember.

Something had changed.

Somewhere between all those little moments…

my perception of you completely changed.

I hadn't expected to fall for someone again. I definitely hadn't expected to trust someone this deeply, not after everything that made trust feel like a bad bet. But the way you showed up — kind, loyal, steady, patient with me in ways I didn't ask for and didn't expect — slowly took apart every reason I had for keeping my guard up. It wasn't sudden. It was gradual, the way morning happens: you don't see it start, you just notice, eventually, that it's light out.

kindness trust comfort respect love

And somehow, you became home in a way I never expected.

I didn't know a flower could become a memory.
